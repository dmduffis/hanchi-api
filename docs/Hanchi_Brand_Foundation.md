# Hanchi Brand Foundation

## Introduction

Hanchi is a platform for discovering the cultures, communities, and stories that shape neighborhoods.

At its core, Hanchi is built on a simple belief: places become meaningful because of the people who live there. Every neighborhood has its own identity, formed over decades through migration, family businesses, traditions, languages, celebrations, and everyday life. These stories are often hidden in plain sight.

Our goal is to help people discover them.

## Why Hanchi

The name Hanchi comes from the Papiamentu word for a small street or alley.

We were initially drawn to the word because of what an alley represents. It is often just around the corner from where we are, yet it is easy to overlook. Some of the most interesting places in a city are not found on the main streets. They are tucked away in side streets where communities have quietly built lives, businesses, and traditions.

While researching the name, we came across _Kada skin’i hanchi_ (“Every Street Corner”), a poem by Curaçaoan poet Pierre Lauffer. The poem reflects on how ordinary street corners become meaningful through the memories attached to them. A place gains significance because of the people who lived there, gathered there, celebrated there, and built a community there.

Although the poem is not about neighborhoods or cultural discovery, its perspective became an inspiration for the brand.

That is what Hanchi aims to uncover.

Not simply places.

But the stories that give those places meaning.

## Vision

To become the world’s cultural map.

We envision a platform where every neighborhood can be understood through the communities that shaped it. Whether someone is exploring their own city or traveling abroad, they should be able to discover the history, traditions, businesses, and people that define a place.

Long term, Hanchi should become the reference point for cultural discovery around the world.

## Mission

Hanchi helps people discover, understand, and support the communities that shape neighborhoods.

We connect users with authentic local businesses, cultural landmarks, historical context, traditions, language, events, and stories so they can experience places with greater understanding and appreciation.

Every feature should encourage curiosity, respect, and meaningful exploration.

## The Problem

Today, people can easily find places.

They can search for restaurants, attractions, hotels, or reviews.

What they cannot easily find is context.

Why is this neighborhood here?

Who built this community?

What traditions are still practiced?

Which businesses have served generations of local families?

What makes this place culturally significant?

These are the questions Hanchi exists to answer.

## Our Approach

Rather than organizing information only by location, Hanchi organizes it around community.

Each neighborhood brings together multiple layers of information:

- History
- Food
- Local businesses
- Community landmarks
- Festivals and events
- Language
- Cultural traditions
- Walking routes
- Stories and local knowledge

Together, these create a richer understanding of place than navigation alone ever could.

## Product Principles

Every decision should support one or more of these principles.

**Understand before recommending.**
Recommendations should explain why a place matters, not simply that it is popular.

**Communities come first.**
Neighborhoods are living communities, not tourist attractions. The product should always represent them with respect and accuracy.

**Support local.**
Independent businesses are often the cultural anchors of a neighborhood. Helping people discover them supports the communities that make these places unique.

**Curiosity over consumption.**
The goal is not to collect places or check off attractions. The goal is to encourage learning, conversation, and appreciation.

**Preserve cultural memory.**
Neighborhoods evolve over time. Their stories, traditions, and businesses deserve to be documented before they disappear.

## What Success Looks Like

A successful Hanchi experience is not measured by how many places someone visits.

It is measured by what they leave understanding.

If someone finishes exploring a neighborhood knowing why it exists, who shaped it, what traditions continue there today, and feeling a stronger connection to the community than when they arrived, then Hanchi has achieved its purpose.

## Long-Term Direction

Hanchi begins with ethnic enclaves because they offer one of the clearest examples of culture shaping place.

Over time, the platform can grow to include historic districts, indigenous communities, regional traditions, immigrant histories, architecture, music, public art, and other forms of cultural identity.

The product may evolve.

The mission should not.

Hanchi exists to help people see the stories that have always been there, often just around the corner.
